# Reading list (2016–2026)

Compiled 2026-10-06 for the 2020/2024 follow-ups to von der Malsburg,
Poppels & Levy (2020, *Psychological Science*).

**How this was compiled.**
- **Forward citations:** I paged through all 6 Google Scholar "Cited by"
  pages for von der Malsburg et al. (2020): 55 entries, about 48 distinct
  works once duplicates are merged (preprint and published versions, and
  three listings of the same AP Stylebook paper). I did the same for all 3
  pages of Kleinman et al. (2022): 24 entries, almost all unrelated COVID
  education or health papers.
- **Keyword searches:** Scholar, limited to 2016 or later, **first page only**
  of each: singular *they* processing; gender stereotypes and pronoun
  resolution; "natural experiment" with language processing; gender-fair
  language reviews; adaptation and expectation updating; female politicians
  and language; Bayesian pronoun production vs. interpretation; Arnold-lab
  work on *they*; Papineau et al.
- **Not done:** the backward reference list of the 2020 paper (mostly
  pre-2016); the 4 citers of its 2018 preprint; any page beyond the first in
  the keyword searches.
- **Not read:** the papers themselves. Notes below come from titles and
  Scholar snippets, plus what is marked as my background knowledge.

## Start here

1. **Ryskin & Fang (2021).** The many timescales of context in language
   processing. *Psychology of Learning and Motivation.* A review of how
   context at different timescales (from the immediate sentence to long-term
   experience) shapes processing. It cites the 2016 study, and it is the
   best framing for "language processing shifts with a changing world".
2. **Kleinman, Morgan, Ostrand & Wittenberg (2022).** Lasting effects of the
   COVID-19 pandemic on language processing. *PLoS One.* A natural
   experiment: a real-world event changed word processing, and the change
   lasted. The closest design analogue to the election studies outside the
   2016 paper.
3. **Pozniak & Burnett (2021).** Failures of Gricean reasoning and the role
   of stereotypes in the production of gender marking in French. *Glossa.*
   A partial replication of von der Malsburg et al. (2020) in French, and so
   far the most direct follow-up in print.
4. **Arnold, Mayo & Dong (2021).** My pronouns are they/them: Talking about
   pronouns changes how pronouns are understood. *Psychonomic Bulletin &
   Review.* Shows that context (here, talking about pronouns) shifts *they*
   comprehension. Read it with **Batista da Costa, Tenenbaum et al. (2024)**,
   "What do they mean?", a systematic review of interpretation, usage and
   acceptability of *they* (*Frontiers in Psychology*). Relevant because
   2024 *they* was read fastest.
5. **Kehler & Rohde (2019).** Prominence and coherence in a Bayesian theory
   of pronoun interpretation. *Journal of Pragmatics.* The standard account
   of why pronoun production and interpretation can come apart (the 2016
   study's production/comprehension dissociation). **Liao, Brochhagen,
   Boleda & Mayol (2025)**, "The role of referent predictability in pronoun
   production: Insights from a Bayesian meta-analysis" (*Language*), is the
   recent quantitative check of it.
6. **Papineau, Podesva & Degen (2022).** "Sally the Congressperson": The
   role of individual ideology on the processing and production of English
   gender-neutral role nouns. *CogSci.* Individual gender ideology predicts
   production of gendered vs. neutral forms; the snippet mentions the 2016
   Clinton expectation. Relevant for whether to model participant ideology
   or election preference.

## A. Language under shifting context and current events

- **Kuperberg & Jaeger (2016).** What do we mean by prediction in language
  comprehension? *Language, Cognition and Neuroscience.* The standard
  review of prediction and adaptation; the background for "expectations →
  processing".
- **Kaan & Chun (2018).** Syntactic adaptation. *Psychology of Learning and
  Motivation.* A review of adapting expectations to the recent environment.
- **Yildirim, Degen, Tanenhaus & Jaeger (2016).** Talker-specificity and
  adaptation in quantifier interpretation. *JML.* Updating expectations about
  language use from experience.
- **Montero-Melis & Jaeger (2020).** Changing expectations mediate
  adaptation in L2 production. *Bilingualism.*
- **Noll, Lowry & Bryant (2018).** Changes over time in the comprehension
  of *he* and *they* as epicene pronouns. *Journal of Psycholinguistic
  Research.* Pronoun interpretation changing over historical time.
- **Ye & Arnold (2026).** People adapt to personal singular *they* through
  exposure. *Open Mind.* Also **Kesan & Arnold (2025, CogSci)**, singular
  *they* exposure increases singular interpretations.
- **James & Rawlins (2024).** Gender-specific and gender-neutral language
  trends in the AP Stylebook and online written news. *English Today.*
  Corpus evidence of shifting usage. Cites the 2016 study.
- **Raet (2023).** Changes in word associations after the COVID-19
  pandemic. *Tertium.* Marginal; event-driven change in associations.
- **Christianson et al. (2022).** What if they're just not that into you
  (or your experiment)? On motivation and psycholinguistics. Cites the 2016
  study (I haven't checked in what context); it is about participant
  engagement with naturalistic vs. decontextualized stimuli.

## B. Gender and pronoun processing

**Reviews and handbook chapters**
- **Arnold (2023).** Pronoun comprehension. *Routledge Handbook of
  Pronouns*, which as a whole (Paterson, ed., 2023) is a good entry point.
- **Collins & Christianson (2024).** The relationship between language and
  bias. *Research Handbook on Language and Bias.* Cites the 2016 study.
- **Sczesny, Formanowicz & Moser (2016).** Can gender-fair language reduce
  gender stereotyping and discrimination? *Frontiers in Psychology.* Highly
  cited review.
- **Lange & von Stockhausen (2025).** What affects people's use of and
  attitudes toward gender-inclusive language? A literature review.
  *Frontiers in Psychology.*

**Singular *they***
- **Arnold, Venkatesh & Vig (2024).** Gender competition in the production
  of nonbinary *they*. *Glossa Psycholinguistics.*
- **Arnold (2026).** Two kinds of singular *they*: A usage-based model.
  *Glossa.*
- **Doherty & Conklin (2017).** How gender-expectancy affects the
  processing of "them". *QJEP.*
- **Moulton et al. (2020).** Singular *they* in context. *Glossa.*
- **Bradley (2020).** The influence of linguistic and social attitudes on
  grammaticality judgments of singular "they". *Language Sciences.*
- **Morris, Snedeker & Prasad (2026).** Distinct ERP signatures for singular
  "they" and gender violations. *Frontiers in Psychology.*
- **Weissman (2026).** Who is [not] expected to use singular *them*?
  *Journal of Cultural Cognitive Science.* Speaker ideology and expectations
  in real-time processing. See also Weissman (2025, *Journal of
  Pragmatics*) on expectations of role-noun use from speaker identity.
- **Bjorkman (2017)** and **Conrod (2019)**: the theoretical syntax of
  singular *they*. Background only.

**Gender stereotypes, bias and reference**
- **Brough, Harris, Wu, Branigan et al. (2024).** Cognitive causes of
  "like me" race and gender biases in human language production. *Nature
  Human Behaviour.* Cites the 2016 study; production biases from the
  speaker's own identity. Relevant to participant gender × pronoun.
- **Lewis & Lupyan (2020).** Gender stereotypes are reflected in the
  distributional structure of 25 languages. *Nature Human Behaviour.* Cites
  the 2016 study.
- **Gardner & Brown-Schmidt (2024).** Biased inferences about gender from
  names. *Glossa Psycholinguistics.* Cites the 2016 study.
- **Redl, Frank, de Swart & de Hoop (2021).** The male bias of a
  generically-intended masculine pronoun: eye-tracking and sentence
  evaluation. *PLoS One.*
- **Hao, He & Fuchs (2024).** Greta is a female director: When gender
  stereotypes interact with informativity expectations. *CogSci.*
- **Xu & Sturt (2023).** The influence of stereotypical information on
  gender inference in Chinese. *SN Social Sciences.* Cites the 2016 study.
- **Prystawski et al. (2022).** The emergence of gender associations in
  child language development. *Cognitive Science.* Cites the 2016 study.
- **Lior & Stanovsky (2023).** Comparing humans and models on a similar
  scale: cognitive gender bias in coreference resolution. arXiv / CogSci.

**Pronoun production vs. interpretation**
- **Hoek, Kehler & Rohde (2021).** Pronominalization and expectations for
  re-mention. *Frontiers in Communication.*
- **Liao, Boleda, Rohde et al. (2024).** Comparing models of pronoun
  production and interpretation via observational and experimental
  evidence. *Glossa.*
- **Lam & Hwang (2025).** Pronoun interpretation is more subject-biased
  than expected by the Bayesian Model. *Language, Cognition and
  Neuroscience.*

**Politics and gender (adjacent; mostly not psycholinguistics)**
- **Jones (2016).** Talk "like a man": The linguistic styles of Hillary
  Clinton, 1992–2013. *Perspectives on Politics.*
- **Cassese et al. (2022).** Media coverage of female candidates' traits in
  the 2020 Democratic presidential primary. *Journal of Women, Politics &
  Policy.*
- **Marjanovic, Stańczak & Augenstein (2022).** Quantifying gender biases
  towards politicians on Reddit. *PLoS One.*
